If you cannot **measure AI project ROI**, you cannot defend the budget—or kill a failing experiment early.

Here is a measurement approach that works for copilots, agents, and internal tools.

## Define the economic story first

Pick one primary outcome:

- Hours saved per week  
- Cost per ticket / lead / invoice  
- Conversion or retention lift  
- Error rate reduction  
- Revenue unlocked (new feature only AI makes possible)  

Secondary metrics are fine. One primary metric keeps debates honest.

## Capture a baseline before launch

Measure two weeks of the current process:

- Time on task  
- Volume  
- Error / rework rate  
- Tooling cost today  

Without a baseline, every demo looks like a win.

## ROI formula that executives accept

A simple model:

> ROI = (Value created − Total AI cost) / Total AI cost

Where **value created** might be:

- Hours saved × fully loaded hourly cost  
- Avoided hire or vendor spend  
- Incremental gross margin from conversion lift  

And **total AI cost** includes:

- Model usage  
- Infra  
- Build/maintain engineering time  
- Reviewer time (humans in the loop)  

## Quality is part of ROI

Cheap answers that cause support escalations are negative ROI. Track:

- Thumbs-up / edit rate  
- Escalation rate vs control group  
- Policy violations caught in review  

## Experiment design beats vibes

- A/B or phased rollout vs control teams  
- Fixed evaluation sets for answer quality  
- Cost dashboards by feature flag  

## Time-to-value matters

Report:

- Weeks to first production use  
- Weeks to positive ROI  
- Payback period  

A project that “will save millions someday” but never ships has infinite payback.

## Bottom line

Measure the ROI of an AI project with baseline → controlled rollout → value vs full cost → quality gates. If those numbers are missing, you are funding a demo—not a business system.
